# Gear Care Guide

## Caring for your tent

Most tent damage happens in storage, not on the trail. After every trip, set the tent up at home or hang it in a shady, ventilated spot and let it dry completely. Even a little moisture trapped in a stuff sack can cause mildew within a few days, and mildew breaks down the waterproof coating. Dry the tent completely before storing it, and keep it loosely packed in a breathable cotton or mesh bag rather than compressed in its small stuff sack. Long-term compression creases the coating and weakens it along the fold lines.

When your tent needs cleaning, use a soft sponge, lukewarm water and a non-detergent soap, and rinse it thoroughly. Never put a tent in a washing machine or dryer, because the agitation and heat will damage the fabric and the seam tape. Avoid bleach, insect repellent and stain removers, which can eat through the waterproof coating.

Sunlight is the other enemy. Ultraviolet light slowly weakens nylon and polyester, so avoid leaving the tent pitched in direct sun for weeks at a time, and use the rainfly as a shade layer when you camp in open terrain. Check the seams at the start of each season. Re-seal the seams every 2 years, or sooner if you see water beading fail or the tape peeling. Clean dirty zippers with an old toothbrush and rub them with a little zipper lubricant if they start to stick.

## Washing sleeping bags

Wash a down sleeping bag only when it is truly dirty or has lost its loft, and no more than once a year. Use a front-loading washer, a down-specific soap, and a gentle cycle with an extra rinse. Dry it on low heat with two or three clean tennis balls to break up clumps, and expect it to take several hours. Synthetic bags can be washed a little more often, but the same gentle approach applies.

## Cleaning and storing packs

Empty your pack completely and shake out crumbs and grit. Scrub stains with a soft brush and mild soap, then hang the pack upside down to dry away from direct sun. Store packs with buckles undone and the straps loose so that the foam and webbing keep their shape.
